Step by step: pick a model

Step 1: Are you counting or measuring?

Counting means how many times something happens: heads, faulty bulbs, made shots. Use binomial.

Measuring means a length, time, weight or test score. Use normal.

Step 2: Check the rules

Binomial needs: a fixed number of tries, only two results (yes or no), the same chance each time, and tries that do not affect each other.

Normal needs: a measurement that is roughly symmetric and bell-shaped.

Step 3: Go to the matching tab

Counting → Binomial tab. Measuring → Normal tab. Not sure? Follow the decision tree picture.

1

Decision: binomial or normal?

What to notice
  • Binomial: a fixed number of tries, two outcomes, the same p, and tries that do not affect each other.
  • Normal: a measurement that is symmetric and bell-shaped.
  • Not every count is binomial; not every measurement is normal.
Quick question: Is the number of heads in 5 coin flips binomial or normal?
Feedback: Binomial. We count heads in 5 flips, the flips do not affect each other, and the chance stays 0.5. Normal is for measurements like delivery times or weights.

What is a probability distribution?

Simple probability gives the chance of one event. Example: flip a fair coin twice. The chance of at least one head is 3/4. That is one number for one question.

A probability distribution lists every possible result and the chance of each one. The results must add up to 1.

Example: flip a fair coin twice and count the heads (X)
Heads (X)012Total
Probability P(X)1/42/41/41

Difference: simple probability answers one question. A distribution shows the whole picture, so you can answer many questions from it. For example, P(at least one head) = P(1) + P(2) = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.

2

Simple probability vs distribution

What to notice
  • Simple probability: one event, one number.
  • Distribution: every possible result, each with its chance, and the chances add up to 1.
  • From a distribution you can get simple probabilities: P(at least one head) = 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.
Quick question: Using the distribution, what is P(exactly 2 heads)?
Feedback: 1/4. Read the right bar: 2 heads has chance 1/4.

Quick memory check

Binomial: X ∼ B(n, p)     Normal: X ∼ N(μ, σ²)

B means “bin” (count of yes results). N means “normal” (a bell-shaped measurement).

Quick guide · Easy steps

Probability made easy 🌱

Binomial in 3 steps

Step 1: Find n, p and k

n = how many tries. p = chance of a “yes” on one try. k = how many “yes” results you want.

Step 2: Count the ways

How many ways can k “yes” results fit into n tries? Write it as ⁿCₖ.

Step 3: Multiply

Multiply the number of ways by p for each yes, and by (1 − p) for each no.

P(X = k) = ⁿCₖ × pk × (1 − p)n−k

Example: 5 coin flips, 2 heads. There are 10 ways, each yes is 0.5 and each no is 0.5.

P(X=2) = 10 × 0.5² × 0.5³ = 0.3125

Normal in 3 steps

Step 1: Find the mean μ

The mean is the centre of the values.

Step 2: Find the SD σ

The SD (standard deviation) tells how spread out the values are. Bigger σ = more spread.

Step 3: Turn x into a z-score

z = (x − μ) / σ tells you how many SDs x is from the mean. Then use the table on the Normal tab.

z = (x − μ) / σ

Example: delivery times with mean 30 minutes and SD 5 minutes. A delivery of 35 minutes has z = 1, so it is one SD above the mean.

z = (35 − 30)/5 = 1

4.4.1–4.4.2 · Counting yes results

4.4.1 Binomial: counting yes results

The question

We flip a fair coin 3 times. What is the chance of getting exactly 2 heads?

Follow the five steps. You can change the numbers in step 5.

Step 1: Count the tries (n)

We flip 3 times, so n = 3.

Step 2: Find the “yes” chance (p)

A fair coin gives heads half the time, so p = 0.5.

Step 3: Choose the “yes” count (k)

We want exactly 2 heads, so k = 2.

1

8 outcomes of three fair coin flips

What to notice
  • There are 8 outcomes, not 16.
  • Each outcome is equally likely.
  • Group the outcomes by the number of heads: 0, 1, 2 or 3.
Quick question: How many sequences have exactly 2 heads?
Feedback: Step 1: list the sequences with 2 heads: HHT, HTH, THH. Step 2: count them: 3. Step 3: 3 out of 8 gives P(X=2) = 3/8.
Step 4: List the outcomes

There are 8 equal outcomes. Find the ones with 2 heads: HHT, HTH and THH. That is 3.

10 heads
31 head
32 heads
13 heads

Each outcome has chance 1/8, so P(X = 2) = 3/8 = 0.375.

2

Probability table and bars

What to notice
  • Each bar is one number of heads, k.
  • All the bars add up to 1.
  • The tallest bar is the most likely number of heads.
Quick question: What is P(X=1), and why are the bars for k=1 and k=2 the same height?
Feedback: Step 1: P(X=1) has 3 sequences, so 3/8. Step 2: P(X=2) also has 3 sequences, so 3/8. The two bars match because p = 0.5.
Step 5: Use the formula

The formula gives the same answer as step 4, without listing every outcome.

P(X = k) = ⁿCₖ × pk × (1 − p)n − k

Put in the numbers: ³C₂ × 0.5² × 0.5¹ = 3 × 0.25 × 0.5 = 0.375.

Try it: change n, p or k.

Bar chart

Each bar is one possible count. The tallest bar is the most likely count. All bars add up to 1.

Why is it ⁿCₖ? (optional)

ⁿCₖ counts the ways to choose which k tries are “yes”. For 3 tries with 2 yes results, ³C₂ = 3: HHT, HTH and THH.

By definition 0! = 1, so ⁿC₀ = 1 (no yes results) and ⁿCₙ = 1 (all yes results).

Let X = number of faulty bulbs, so X ∼ B(20, 0.05). Find P(X = 1).

4.4.3–4.4.5 · Measurements

4.4.3 Normal: measuring values

The question

A delivery takes on average 30 minutes, with a spread (SD) of 5 minutes. A delivery takes 35 minutes. How far is that from the average, and how often is a delivery this fast or faster?

Follow the four steps. You can change the numbers in each step.

Step 1: Know the mean (μ) and SD (σ)

The mean is the centre of the bell. The SD is how wide the bell is.

For the delivery example: μ = 30 and σ = 5.

Rule of thumb

About 68% of values are within 1 SD of the mean, 95% within 2 SDs, and 99.7% within 3 SDs.

1

Normal curve: mean, SD bands, empirical rule

What to notice
  • 68% lie within one standard deviation of the mean.
  • 95% lie within two standard deviations.
  • 99.7% lie within three standard deviations.
Quick question: What percentage of marks are between 60 and 80 if μ=70 and σ=10?
Feedback: Step 1: 70 − 10 = 60 and 70 + 10 = 80. Step 2: the area between 60 and 80 is about 68%.
Step 2: Turn the value into a z-score

z tells you how many SDs a value is from the mean.

2a: Subtract the mean

35 − 30 = 5 minutes.

2b: Divide by the SD

5 ÷ 5 = 1.

z = (x − μ) / σ   →   z = (35 − 30) / 5 = 1

So 35 minutes is 1 SD above the mean.

Try it: change μ, σ or x.

What the z-score means

z = 0 is the mean. z = 1 is one SD above it. z = −1 is one SD below it.

Once you have z, the table gives the chance of a value that is that small or smaller.

Step 3: Read the table

The table gives Φ(z): the chance of a value at or below z. Look at z = 1.00 for the delivery example:

  1. Row: find 1.0 down the side.
  2. Column: find .00 across the top.
  3. Read: the value is 0.8413.
P(X ≤ 35) = Φ(1) = 0.8413

So about 84% of deliveries take 35 minutes or less.

3

Normal table lookup: how to read the table

What to notice
  • Step 1: find the row 1.2.
  • Step 2: find the column .03.
  • Step 3: read 0.8907 where they meet.
  • Some tables show the right side instead. Check the heading first.
Quick question: What is Φ(1.23) from this table?
Feedback: Φ(1.23) = 0.8907. This means about 89% of the area lies to the left of z = 1.23.
Show the full standard normal table

Find the row (z to one decimal), then the column (the second decimal).

Step 4: Shade the area

The shaded area is the chance. It is the area to the left of your value.

Left area = Φ(z)     Right area = 1 − Φ(z)

For a measurement, one exact value has chance 0, so “less than” and “at most” give the same answer.
4

Left, right and between areas on the normal curve

What to notice
  • Left area = P(X≤x).
  • Right area = P(X≥x)=1−Φ(z).
  • Between area = P(a≤X≤b)=Φ(z_b)−Φ(z_a).
Quick question: What is the difference between P(X≤35) and P(25≤X≤35)?
Feedback: P(X≤35) is the whole area left of 35; P(25≤X≤35) is only the area between 25 and 35. Do not mix up the areas under the curve.

More practice (open when ready)

Practice 1: test scores (mean 70, SD 5)

Find z for a score of 75: z = (75 − 70) / 5 = 1. So 75 is one SD above the mean.

2

Raw values and z-scores

What to notice
  • The raw value and the z-score represent the same point.
  • z is the number of standard deviations from the mean.
  • A z-score makes it easy to compare two different scales.
Quick question: What is z for x=75 if μ=70 and σ=5?
Feedback: Step 1: 75 − 70 = 5. Step 2: 5 ÷ 5 = 1. So 75 is 1 SD above the mean.
Practice 2: delivery times, both sides

Left area: P(X ≤ 35) = Φ(1) = 0.8413. Right area: P(X ≥ 35) = 1 − 0.8413 = 0.1587.

Between 25 and 35: z = −1 and z = 1, so Φ(1) − Φ(−1) = 0.8413 − 0.1587 = 0.6827.

Practice 3: which result is better?

Compare each score with its own mean using z.

Test 1: score 75, mean 70, SD 5. z = (75 − 70) / 5 = 1.00
Test 2: score 45, mean 40, SD 4. z = (45 − 40) / 4 = 1.25

Test 2 is better, because its score is further above its own mean.

Advanced · Ranges of values

Beyond “exactly one value”

The question

We flip a fair coin 3 times (n = 3, p = 0.5). X = number of heads.

Each step asks a different kind of question. Follow them in order.

Step 1: Exactly k

“Exactly 2 heads” means only the bar at k = 2.

P(X = 2) = 3/8 = 0.375

This is one bar from the chart.

1

Binomial bars: exactly, at most, at least

What to notice
  • "Exactly 2" = only k=2.
  • "At most 2" = add k=0, 1, 2.
  • "At least 2" = add k=2, 3.
Quick question: If X is the number of heads in 3 flips, what is P(X≤2)?
Feedback: Step 1: for at most 2, add the bars for 0, 1 and 2: 1/8 + 3/8 + 3/8 = 7/8. Step 2: for at least 2, add the bars for 2 and 3: 3/8 + 1/8 = 1/2.
Step 2: At most k (add from the left)

“At most 2 heads” means 0, 1 or 2 heads. Add those bars.

P(X ≤ 2) = P(0) + P(1) + P(2) = 1/8 + 3/8 + 3/8 = 7/8
Step 3: At least k (add from the right)

“At least 2 heads” means 2 or 3 heads.

P(X ≥ 2) = P(2) + P(3) = 3/8 + 1/8 = 1/2
Step 4: Between a and b

“Between 1 and 2 heads” means add the bars for 1 and 2.

P(1 ≤ X ≤ 2) = 3/8 + 3/8 = 3/4

Try it: change n, p and the bounds a and b. The results update.

Read the picture

Exactly 2 is one bar. At most 2 adds the bars for 0, 1 and 2. At least 2 adds the bars for 2 and 3.

Step 5: A range of normal values

Change both ends to z first, then subtract the two areas.

P(a ≤ X ≤ b) = Φ(zb) − Φ(za)

Example: mean 30, SD 5. For 25 to 35: z = −1 and z = 1, so Φ(1) − Φ(−1) = 0.8413 − 0.1587 = 0.6827.

Try it: change μ, σ and the ends of the range.

Tips to avoid mistakes

Five common mistakes

  1. Using variance instead of SD: for z, divide by σ, not σ². If σ² = 25, then σ = 5.
  2. Mixing up “exactly” and “at most”: P(X ≤ 3) adds the chances for 0, 1, 2 and 3.
  3. Reading the wrong side: Φ(z) is the area on the left. For the right side, use 1 − Φ(z).
  4. Mixing up success and failure: use p for yes and 1 − p for no.
  5. Forgetting independence: picking with replacement keeps tries independent, so binomial works.

Practice questions

Try each one, then open the answer.

1. 4 coin flips, p = 0.5. Is this binomial?

Yes. Fixed tries, two outcomes, same p, independent flips.

2. Heights of students. Binomial or normal?

Normal, because height is a measurement.

Binomial, because we count faulty bulbs.

P(0) = 1/4 and P(1) = 2/4. Add them: 3/4.

Summary · Your revision sheet

Probability Lab summary

Use this as a quick reference. Each formula has a plain meaning and an example.

Binomial: count yes results

Use when: a fixed number n of tries, two outcomes, the same chance p, and independent tries.
P(X = k) = ⁿCₖ × pk × (1 − p)n − k
E(X) = np     Var(X) = np(1 − p)

E(X) is the average count. Var(X) shows how spread out the counts are.

Normal: measure values

Use when: a measurement is roughly symmetric and bell-shaped.
z = (x − μ) / σ   →   P(X ≤ x) = Φ(z)
P(a ≤ X ≤ b) = Φ(zb) − Φ(za)

Φ(z) means the chance of a value at or below z. Change each end to z first, then use the table.

Three binomial examples

  • Basketball: 10 shots, 70% each. The average is 7 makes, but any number from 0 to 10 can happen.
  • Bulbs: 20 bulbs, 5% faulty each. P(exactly 1 faulty) ≈ 0.3774.
  • Coins: 5 flips, exactly 3 heads. P = 10 × 0.5⁵ = 0.3125.

Three normal examples

If the measurement is normal:

  • Test scores: mean 70, SD 10. Scores 60 to 80 are within 1 SD: about 68%.
  • Delivery: mean 30 minutes, SD 5. Within 35 minutes is z = 1: about 84%.
  • Height: mean 165 cm, SD 8 cm. Taller than 173 cm is z = 1: about 16%.
